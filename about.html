Who we are

we grew up here

Turning Life On is a Massachusetts-based coalition helping communities shift the norms around technology. What sets us apart is where we came from: we didn't parachute into Massachusetts, we grew up here. Since 2017, our grassroots start has become a network of experienced, in-state community leads who know the unique challenges and opportunities in Massachusetts communities and districts because we've lived them.

We back local groups with playbooks, funding, connections and expert support, so more kids get room to learn, play and connect in the real world.

Watch: It Happens Here: Concord's Push To Revolutionize Kids And Phones, October 2018. A WBZ-TV interview about how we started.

Why communities turn to us

nearly a decade of groundwork

Here's how our experience supports your work:

  • Direct lines to decision-makers. We work with the Massachusetts Attorney General's Office, the State House and the Governor's Office, and were invited to join Governor Healey for her announcement of new social media protections for kids in May 2026. Read our Meta Settlement briefing
  • Leaders who've done this work. Our board includes a founding member of the Children's Screen Time Action Network's Screens in Schools Working Group, a former co-lead of the Distraction Free Schools Policy Project, a founding member and former co-lead of Smartphone Free Childhood U.S., and a Smartphone Free Childhood U.S. board member. Our leaders have founded local groups in Arlington, Concord, Lexington and the Nashoba Valley and have experience in education, technology, communication and nonprofits.
  • National partners. Fairplay, The Anxious Generation, the Four Norms Leadership Circle and Culture Reframed, to name a few, work with us to bring the latest research and strategy to our local leads.
  • Playbooks built here. Tested in real Massachusetts districts, not adapted from somewhere else.
  • Funding for local groups. We raise money so community leads can pay for rooms, speakers and events, and we're dedicated to growing our philanthropic base to support even more communities.
  • A network of experienced leads. People who know the challenges in Massachusetts, and the openings too.

A statewide coalition moves faster. When one community figures something out, every other community benefits.

What we believe

a continuum, not a switch

Technology use exists on a continuum, from constant, compulsive engagement to complete disconnection. Our goal is to help families, schools, and communities move toward a healthier, more intentional, and age-appropriate relationship with tech, regardless of where they are starting from.

We are not anti-tech. We recognize what technology makes possible, and we meet people where they are, supporting any step toward balance.

Our team

led by parents who do this work in their own Massachusetts communities, too

Adrienne Principe

Founder and President · Concord

A certified Child and Teen Coach, Adrienne blends the latest research on child development with real-world parenting wisdom to offer practical, values-based strategies for healthy tech use. She co-founded the Concord Promise, serves on the MA Distraction Free Schools Policy Project, is a founding member of the Children's Screen Time Action Network's Screens in Schools Working Group, and sits on the Emerson Health Foundation Board of Directors. Adrienne lives in Concord with her husband, their four busy and very different kids, and their dog, Gus.

Emily Lines

Vice President · Concord

Emily founded the Intentional Tech Coalition in Concord and is a dedicated advocate for community-based partnerships that help parents, caregivers and schools make intentional, developmentally aligned technology decisions. She's deeply committed to protecting childhood, supporting mental health, and strengthening family and community connections. Emily holds a master's degree in Animals and Public Policy and brings a background in finance, nonprofit leadership and strategic consulting. She lives in Concord with her husband, children, and rescued pit bulls.

Local group: Intentional Tech Coalition

Wendi Hoffer

Director of Community Engagement · Lexington

Wendi founded Lex Kids Be Kids and is a passionate advocate for a more human, less digital childhood. At Turning Life On, she builds the framework that connects community leads so they can support one another, a network that has proven especially valuable when advocating for change with school districts. Wendi works for a separate nonprofit and has led one, and she channels that experience into meaningful change for families and vulnerable communities. She lives in Lexington with her husband and three diversely-abled children.

Local group: Lex Kids Be Kids

Jeff Miller

Treasurer and AI Specialist · Arlington

Jeff founded Tech-Wise Arlington, a parent-to-parent guide that helps local families think through smartphones, phone-free schools, classroom tech and AI, including a plain-language guide to AI for parents. He's an advocate for Wait Until 8th and phone-free schools and works to share ideas and resources across communities. Jeff is EVP of Strategy and Acquisition at Acxion Foodservice and has served on 10 corporate boards. He also sits on the boards of Smartphone Free Childhood U.S., the Arlington Libraries Foundation, and the Humanist Chaplaincy at Harvard and MIT. Jeff holds an A.B. in Economics from Harvard and lives in Arlington with his wife, two children, and two dogs.

Local group: Tech-Wise Arlington

Kelsey Magnuson

Director of Development · Bolton

Kelsey is a public health professional with more than 10 years of experience translating community data into strategic programming that improves health, including local Youth Risk Behavior Survey data showing growing concern about youth mental health. She is passionate about bringing people together for shared learning and program development. Kelsey first worked with Turning Life On in 2020, developing an educational campaign through website content and a mini podcast series, and as Director of Development she now leads TLO's fundraising through grants and sponsorships. She holds an MPH and lives in Bolton with her husband and two children.

JH

Jennifer Henderson

Special Projects · Stow

Jennifer founded Reconnect Nashoba Valley and is a certified Digital Wellbeing coach committed to changing the norms around tech use. Since beginning her advocacy work in 2021, she has served as co-lead of the Distraction Free Schools Policy Project and was a founding member and co-lead of Smartphone Free Childhood U.S. Jennifer is Head of Supply Chain at a rare disease pharmaceutical company. She lives in Stow with her husband, daughter, and her two dogs.

Local group: Reconnect Nashoba Valley

Sheila Pundit

Device-Free Events · Sudbury

Sheila works with Sudbury for Digital Balance and spent many years as a teacher and administrator in public schools, after-school programs and summer programs. She was Executive Director of Summer Ink, a Boston nonprofit creative writing program that welcomes middle schoolers from a wide range of backgrounds, and helped design K–12 English Language Arts curriculum at the Writers' Express, later acquired by the education technology company Amplify. Sheila serves on the board of the Goodnow Library Foundation, which supports Sudbury's Goodnow Library, and is active in The Reconnect Movement, which creates more screen-free social time for all ages.

Local group: Sudbury for Digital Balance
