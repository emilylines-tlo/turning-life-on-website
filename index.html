Keep tech in check.

A question of when, how much, and who benefits. Here is where each one stands, and what communities across Massachusetts are doing about it.

Smartphones & social

Our attention is the product.

Phones displace face-to-face time, because the apps on them earn money by holding attention.

Good news

Meta settled with state attorneys general for $17.1 billion over designing its apps to addict young users. Massachusetts gets at least $366 million.

Massachusetts Attorney General, August 2026

Read the case →

Phone-free schools

The school day works better without them.

Bell-to-bell policies give teachers back the room, and give kids back each other at lunch.

Good news

More than thirty states now require phone-free school days. Massachusetts has passed a bill in both chambers — the two versions have yet to be reconciled.

Stateline, March 2026 · Boston Globe, September 2026

Where the bill stands →

Screens in schools

A decade of promises, a decade of decline.

Devices arrived in classrooms on a promise of better learning. Reading scores are down in 83% of districts compared with a decade ago.

Good news

Districts are pulling back. Cambridge told families this September that technology should not replace relationships, physical activity, reading, writing and creative work, and tightened its device guidance to match.

Cambridge Public Schools, September 2026 · Stanford Educational Opportunity Project, 2026

Read more →

AI & childhood

Powerful, and not yet safe for kids.

Using AI well takes the judgment to check what comes back, which is exactly what children are still building.

Good news

Cambridge now blocks generative AI from pre-K through 5th grade. Three in four US parents support pausing it in schools.

Cambridge Public Schools, September 2026 · YouGov for the Institute for Family Studies, July 2026
