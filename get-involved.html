Learn more about AI and skip AI companion apps for kids.

Apps like Character.AI, Replika, Chai and Talkie are built to keep users talking. They replace the pushback that real relationships provide with constant validation. A friend or parent will tell a kid when an idea is unkind, unsafe or wrong. These apps, by design, agree.

What Parents Need to Know About AI

Teach kids that AI lies confidently.

AI sounds fluent. It cites things. It uses authoritative language. None of that means it's right, and the most dangerous outputs are the wrong ones that feel correct. Kids need a working assumption: check it before you trust it.

Check the smart speaker and the toys.

The chatbot may already be in the kitchen. AI is no longer only something kids open on a laptop. Amazon recently updated Alexa into a full chatbot built on the same kind of technology as ChatGPT, and AI plush toys are sold as a child's best friend. Check what your speakers and toys can do, and decide whether you want them in conversation with your kids.

Ask your school about AI.

Which AI tools are students using, what data do they collect, and has anyone shown they help kids learn?

Start a conversation with your building principal or district leadership, and ask for evidence of learning.

Approval on the privacy list means a tool cleared data-privacy review. It does not mean anyone has shown it improves learning. Almost no AI tool used in K–12 has independent evidence that it does. For every approved AI tool, ask: Why this tool? What learning outcome is it improving? How will we know it's working?

Advocate for a pause on AI in your district.

New York City and Los Angeles have both paused student AI use until it can be tested for safety, efficacy, equity and privacy. There's never been a better time to ask for one in your district.
