Small steps that make a difference

Things you can do at home and at your child's school. Every conversation and every small change is a step in the right direction.

Create a family media planDecide together what tech looks like in your home, before the arguments start.

School should be the easy part.

Ask one question at pickup"How much screen time does my child get in a typical school day?" One curious question shows your school that families care.
Tell the State House: advance phone-free schools nowBoth chambers have voted for bell-to-bell, phone-free schools, but the final bill is stuck in conference committee. Email the negotiators and your own legislators, and ask them to finish the job.
Ask your teacher to ditch free screen-timeAn email or conversation to request students in your child's class engage in screen-free activities during free time.
Request informed consentMost schools ask for your consent for your child's internet and device use, often buried in an annual form. You can give consent based on conditions.

Powerful, and unsafe for kids. AI is arriving in classrooms faster than anyone can study it.

Learn more about AI, and skip AI companion apps for kidsApps like Character.AI, Replika, Chai and Talkie are built to keep users talking. They replace the pushback real relationships provide with constant validation. A friend or parent will tell a kid when an idea is unkind, unsafe or wrong. These apps, by design, agree.
Teach kids that AI lies confidentlyAI sounds fluent. It cites things. It uses authoritative language. None of that means it's right, and the most dangerous outputs are the wrong ones that feel correct. Kids need a working assumption: check it before you trust it.
Check the smart speaker and the toysThe chatbot may already be in the kitchen. Amazon has updated Alexa into a full chatbot built on the same kind of technology as ChatGPT, and AI plush toys are sold as a child's best friend. Check what your speakers and toys can do, and decide whether you want them in conversation with your kids.
Ask your school about AIWhich AI tools are students using, what data do they collect, and has anyone shown they help kids learn?
Sign and share the petition to pause AI in schoolsUntil AI tools are independently proven safe, effective, equitable and private, they don't belong with students.

Pick your trailWe've sorted nine school policy priorities by difficulty, like ski trails. Green circle for easy wins, blue square to pick up the pace, black diamond for steep and technical. Start wherever your district is, and climb at your own pace.
Connect with IT about how students get around filtersKids finding workarounds isn't a reason to abandon defaults, filtering or device restrictions. It's part of why layered guardrails matter in the first place. A conversation with IT can be a great first step.
Public records requestIf you've been at this a while and you're still not getting anywhere with your district, it might be time for a request to get real answers.

Powerful, and unsafe for kids. AI is arriving in classrooms and in homes faster than anyone can study it. The results aren't good.

Sign and share the petition to pause AI in schoolsUntil AI tools are independently proven safe, effective, equitable and private, they don't belong with students.
Start a conversation with your principal and ask for evidence of learningApproval on the privacy list means a tool cleared data-privacy review. It does not mean anyone has shown it improves learning. For every approved AI tool, ask: why this tool? What learning outcome is it improving? How will we know it's working?
Advocate for a pause on AI in your districtNew York City and Los Angeles have both paused generative AI in schools while they work out safety, efficacy, equity and privacy. There's never been a better time to ask for one in your district.
Get parents a seat at the tableA Tech Advisory Committee and digital product standards give families a real voice in which AI tools reach classrooms.
